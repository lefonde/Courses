First-week lessons: acceptance and scoring standard

This is the fixed standard for revising and evaluating the four first-week lessons. The final report must use these criteria, disclose failures and limitations, and provide evidence for each lesson's score. Scores evaluate the material and its delivery, not the learner, an expected exam grade, or proven educational effectiveness.

Governing sources: the repository's Development Protocol (16 October 2026), the agreed tutoring policy and its September trial revision, and the user's current corrections: concrete Hebrew, purposeful questions, a real opportunity to attempt the problem, and preservation of existing progress. Where a general template conflicts with those explicit preferences, the preferences govern.

2. Non-negotiable acceptance gates

  1. Mathematical and source integrity. No known material error in definitions, assumptions, proof, answer key, boundary case or verbal interpretation. Verify official restrictions against actual source documents; do not silently replace them with an invented question or imply that an authored exercise is official.
  2. Attempt before disclosure. On any practice or retrieval page, its own solution, decisive intermediate values, chosen event decomposition and selected proof route are not visible before a deliberate help reveal. Defining a problem and teaching a prerequisite are allowed. A worked example is explicitly identified and must not solve a later exercise that is presented as a first independent attempt. If the same official work has necessarily been demonstrated, label reconstruction or assisted practice honestly.
  3. Progress preservation. No reset, overwrite, deletion or automatic reinterpretation of existing drafts, reading positions, help exposure, completion, learned-topic flags, attempts, schedules, history or linked PDFs. Existing IDs and storage keys remain compatible. If a question's mathematical task changes, preserve the old prompt and attempt with its revision; never silently attach that draft to a different question. Prefer changes that retain the same experiment and target. Editorial revision metadata must not make old records unreadable.
  4. Truthful interaction. Reading, hints, solution reveals and edits do not confer independent-solution credit. No forced quiz, mandatory reconstruction, extra exercise or artificial hint ladder to finish or obtain a requested solution. Save failures retain work and report failure. No automatic transmission to an external tutor.
  5. Usable essential flow. A learner can read the question, enter or use a paper answer, reveal the chosen level of help, move between sections and return. No broken required navigation, hidden question parts, unreadable formulas or inaccessible essential controls in the tested layouts.

A failed gate blocks acceptance regardless of the numerical score. An unperformed essential check is “not checked,” not a pass. A remaining unavailable human/device check must be reported with its practical limit; it cannot be represented as observed learner success.

3. Weighted editorial rubric (100 points per lesson)

Each row has four equally weighted checks. Rate each check 0–4: 0 absent or seriously wrong; 1 major gaps; 2 mixed, with recurring material weaknesses; 3 sound with a specific minor weakness; 4 consistently meets the criterion in every applicable section. A single minor issue does not invalidate an otherwise sound lesson, but repeated issues must reduce the rating. Row points = weight × mean check rating ÷ 4. Round the final total to one decimal. No discretionary bonus points.

A. Clear Hebrew and notation

A2 Every experiment, event, variable and referent is explicit where needed; no “copied tosses” shorthand.
A3 Symbols, terminology and log bases introduced before use; no ambiguity between events and their indicators.
A4 Questions and summaries can be understood without guessing what the author intended or searching for an unnamed earlier example.

15		B1 Each question asks for an identifiable mathematical product linked to the outcome.
B2 Learner makes a substantive decision, interpretation, justification or calculation; no disguised arithmetic labeling or trivial blank filling.
B3 Complete context and assumptions; multiple parts clearly identified and proportionate.
B4 Feedback explains what establishes understanding, common errors and valid alternatives; no generic “correct” claim based on an unseen answer.
C. Ownership of reasoning
20		C1 Initial practice text does not supply the target solution or crucial intermediate values.
C2 Titles, objectives, preceding examples, checks and AI payloads do not covertly reveal an uncued target method.
C3 Guided work allows a meaningful multi-step attempt; independent work removes unsolicited cues.
C4 Method restrictions genuinely required by the source remain; named-method drills and reconstruction are labeled honestly rather than credited as independent method selection.
D. Explanations and progression
15		D1 Concrete situation → concept/notation → reason for calculation; sufficient prerequisites and repair route.
D2 Important mathematical transitions are explained, not just listed as equalities.
D3 Examples support later practice without doing it in advance; help fades and transfer changes something meaningful.
D4 Summaries state useful conclusions and unresolved work; optional reflection is concrete, answerable and supported, never compulsory repetition.
E. Mathematical correctness and sources
15		E1 Definitions, formulas, calculations and solutions independently checked.
E2 Assumptions and edge cases stated (independence, finite moments, ranges, rounding).
E3 Words match the mathematics: upper bound versus probability, sufficient versus optimal, polynomial versus exponential, dependence versus zero covariance.
E4 Source records accurate; official scope intact; authored preparation distinguished from official questions and solutions.
F. Graduated help and useful feedback
10		F1 First hint identifies an accessible idea or obstacle without handing over the numerical answer.
F2 Later hints add support in distinct useful increments and remain optional.
F3 Full solution is available directly on request and explains choices, assumptions and calculations.
F4 Checking criteria address likely reasoning errors and assistance honestly, allowing valid alternative solutions.
G. External tutoring consistency
5		G1 Copied request matches the viewed section and selected help mode.
G2 Correct activity policy for concept, demonstration, practice, retrieval or summary; no conflicting mandatory opening question.
G3 Necessary context and precise source identification included, hidden hints/solutions and private draft excluded unless deliberately selected.
G4 Hebrew, direct teaching for missing knowledge, meaningful attempts, request-only help where appropriate, accurate reporting and optional ending activities preserved.
H. Presentation and usable flow
5		H1 Explanation/question/hint/solution/source visually and semantically distinct.
H2 Hebrew RTL and equations LTR; narrow-screen reflow and localized long-equation scrolling.
H3 Clear response method, optional paper work, honest save/assessment wording and keyboard-accessible reveals.
H4 Stable direct routes, navigation and stop/return flow; time estimates consistent with unchanged session allocation.

Acceptance threshold: each lesson ≥85/100, every dimension averaging at least 3/4, and all applicable gates passing. Report each lesson separately; the four-lesson mean must not conceal a weak lesson. A 100 would mean no identified editorial shortcoming under this rubric, not proof of learning effectiveness.

4. Required checks and evidence

  1. Freeze the baseline. Record the repository commit, all lesson/section IDs and questions, storage compatibility and protected-file hashes. Save this standard before editing lessons; record its hash. Any necessary later amendment must be explicit and must not lower the bar to excuse a finding.
  2. Whole-lesson review. Read all four lessons end to end. Maintain a section inventory with issues, revisions and final verdicts. Inspect initial content separately from revealed hints and solutions. Review Hebrew aloud conceptually: every sentence must identify its subject and teaching purpose.
  3. Mathematical verification. Independently check proofs and compare source restrictions. Enumerate small coin/permutation examples and verify numerical bounds when useful; do not treat finite enumeration as proof of an asymptotic claim.
  4. Prompt review. Generate requests for every section and both help modes. Check that mode selection actually works, no solution leaks through context, official references survive, current section is used and reporting/JSON does not override teaching. Test demonstrated and summary activities separately.
  5. Progress regression. Use synthetic pre-update state with nonempty drafts, bookmark, hint/solution records, completion, learned flags, attempts and schedule overrides. Compare through reading, rerender, save, reload and export/import. Verify old content versions remain readable, unrelated fields remain identical, help exposure is monotonic and failures/conflicts preserve work. Never use or import the user's real progress as a fixture.
  6. Rendered inspection. Review every section in a local preview. Inspect representative desktop and narrow layouts, mixed Hebrew/formulas, hint/full-solution reveals, navigation and keyboard actions. Record browser, dimensions and untested environments. The public origin's personal storage must not be touched for testing.
  7. Fresh evaluation. A reviewer who did not author the content applies this exact rubric, with independent mathematics/technical checks. Fix failures and rescore the affected material; report the final version and any remaining deductions. Reviewer agreement is evidence of review, not human learning validation.
  8. Required project checks. Run the repository's test command and focused regressions justified by the changed behavior. Inspect the final diff and publication payload for unrelated or personal files.

5. Final report contract

Deliver a readable report containing: baseline and evaluated commit/revision; this standard and its unchanged hash; all four lesson briefs; a complete section-by-section audit; before/after examples; criterion ratings and weighted scores with evidence and deductions; every acceptance gate; exact tests and results; source verification; progress compatibility; what was prepared, verified, published and checked live; remaining limitations and a concise optional learner check. Include failures rather than hiding them behind test counts. Clearly separate automated facts, editorial judgments and unobserved learner outcomes.

Deployment is a separate factual status. If published within the user's request to fix the website, record the deployment commit and verify that exact public assets arrived. If publication is blocked, deliver the evaluated local result and identify the concrete blocker. No score may imply that a new external tutor conversation, real mobile device, screen reader or uncoached learner attempt was observed unless it actually was.
