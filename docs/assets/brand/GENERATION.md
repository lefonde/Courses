The owner asked to preserve age by changing the length, direction and unevenness of v19's existing strokes, without adding wrinkles. Both new passes used v19 and the original photograph as references. The owner subsequently selected [v25](erdos-proof-v25.png) for the site. v19 remains the preserved reference for this study.

The main visible changes in v25 are flatter, uneven under-eye folds and longer, unequal cheek strokes. The upper lids and brows stay close to v19 despite the broader prompt. The separate nose, unequal eye openings, open mouth and shallow lower-lip curve remain. Visual review found no additional wrinkle contours. An earlier pass, v24, made the under-eye contours too decorative. Both images and prompts are archived under `outputs/brand-studies/erdos/v19-age-studies/`.

These opaque artwork studies were made with the built-in image generator. Their subsequent site integration is recorded above.

### v25 prompt · built-in image generator

Create ONE restrained revision of IMAGE 1 (v19). IMAGE 2 is the original photograph of Paul Erdos and must determine the man's age and expression. Black ink on plain opaque white, same square composition, no text.

Keep v19's exact minimalist feature arrangement and the same number of contours. The requested change is OLD SKIN'S WEIGHT SUGGESTED BY EXISTING LINE SHAPES, without a single additional wrinkle. Preserve the separation of glasses and nose, the open crooked smile, the two cheek folds and the shallow lower-lip boundary. No deletion or further abstraction.

Give the existing eye/cheek/brow lines the restrained irregularity of an old face observed from life:
1. UNDER EACH EYE: replace the tidy smile-shaped arc with one very shallow, slightly drooping uneven fold. Let a flatter middle stretch turn down briefly off centre and taper softly back up. Unequal contours, gently angular in one place rather than perfect crescents. Make the turns and weight follow the photographed eye bags. No upward-hooked endpoints, flourishes, multiple strokes or parallel wrinkles. Each remains ONE line.
2. EYELIDS: preserve viewer-left almost closed and viewer-right visibly more open. The existing upper lids should sit heavily and a little unevenly, with a gentle downward outer-end slope as in the photograph. Keep the amused squint. Do not make the eyes equal or remove the right iris. Suggest relaxation and wear, not sadness.
3. CHEEK FOLDS: retain the two separate lines beside the smile, but let their lower ends reach subtly farther down, following the photographed hanging cheeks. Use a slight change of direction midway, unequal length and restrained weight changes. They should feel like folds in relaxed elderly skin, not symmetric brackets framing a cartoon mouth.
4. BROWS: maintain the unequal raised shape, but subtly flatten one stretch of each and vary the taper. No added hair lines.

The change should be visible yet small. This remains v19 with age in its line anatomy. Keep the spectacles, long separate nose, mouth opening and beloved shallow lower-lip curve as close as possible to v19. Do not enlarge the grin or regularize the face. Keep his tired laughter, warmth and peculiar asymmetry.
No hair, head outline, ears, forehead wrinkles, new wrinkle marks, sketch texture, roughened surfaces, crosshatching, stippling, jagged noise, tremor imitation, graffiti, cute mascot or stock vector-emoji styling. Avoid ornamental calligraphic swooshes. Thin purposeful ink that feels observed rather than geometrically polished.
